## Issues Lincoln raised
* [high] Specialty occupation not shown. The stated minimum requirement is an 'Assistant Nursing degree' plus experience, and no job description exists beyond the title. An H-1B requires a position that normally needs a bachelor's degree or higher in a specific specialty, and nursing assistant roles ordinarily do not. The employer's reliance on 'medicine and health' in 8 CFR 214.2(h)(4)(ii) (a definition of specialty occupation) does not make a job a specialty occupation by field alone.
* [high] Worker's credential is below a bachelor's degree. The worker has a Syrian two-year associate degree after secondary baccalaureate. The claim that no educational difference exists between an associate's and a bachelor's degree in Syria is unsupported, and no credential evaluation is in the file. Equivalence through experience would need a separate evaluation and a qualifying position, and the position itself is the first problem.
* [high] Cap exemption claimed without a basis. The employer says the filing is cap-exempt but gives no basis. A physician's practice is not shown to be an institution of higher education, a related or affiliated nonprofit, or a nonprofit research organization, and no concurrent employment or prior cap-counted status is stated. Absent a basis, the petition would be cap-subject and would need a registration selection.
* [medium] Facts and documents needed. Missing: worker's name, current status, location and I-94; passport; diploma and transcripts with certified translations; work experience letters; any credential evaluation; detailed job description and duties; employer's name, EIN and organization type; offered wage; proposed start date; Labor Condition Application; and any H-1B registration or prior cap-counted H-1B history.
* [medium] Employer unrepresented and relying on own legal theory. The employer is not represented and has built its position on a regulatory reading that appears unsound. The attorney should advise on this before any filing, and should consider whether another category could fit.

### Requirement 1: The position is a specialty occupation
Governing rule: INA 214(i)(1) and 8 CFR 214.2(h)(4)(ii) define a specialty occupation as one requiring theoretical and practical application of a body of highly specialized knowledge and attainment of a bachelor's degree or higher in a specific specialty, or its equivalent, as a minimum for entry. The petitioner must also satisfy one of the criteria at 8 CFR 214.2(h)(4)(iii)(A).

Assessment: Not met on the present file. The employer's stated minimum requirement is an 'Assistant Nursing degree' with experience, which is not shown to be a bachelor's degree in a specific specialty (p. 1). No job description or duties exist beyond the title 'nursing assistant' (p. 1). Without duties, none of the criteria in 8 CFR 214.2(h)(4)(iii)(A) can be assessed.

The employer argues that 'medicine and health' is listed in 8 CFR 214.2(h)(4)(ii), so the job is a specialty occupation (p. 1). That is the employer's own statement of its legal theory (p. 1). The listing of a field in the definition does not by itself make every job in that field a specialty occupation; the position must itself require a bachelor's degree or higher in a specific specialty. [Attorney: Confirm this reading of the regulation and advise the employer in writing.]

[Attorney: Nursing assistant positions ordinarily do not require a bachelor's degree. Consider whether the Occupational Outlook Handbook or O*NET supports the position, once a job description is obtained.]

### Requirement 2: The worker holds the required degree or its equivalent
Governing rule: 8 CFR 214.2(h)(4)(iii)(C) requires a U.S. bachelor's degree or higher in the specialty, a foreign degree equivalent to it, or an unrestricted license, or education, specialized training and experience equivalent to such a degree.

Assessment: Not met on the present file. The worker holds a Syrian two-year associate degree after a secondary baccalaureate (p. 1). The employer states that 'there is no educational difference between an Associate's and a Bachelor's degree in the country of Syria' (p. 1). Nothing in the file supports that statement, and no credential evaluation exists. [Attorney: Obtain a credential evaluation of the diploma and transcripts. A two-year program would ordinarily be unlikely to be evaluated as a U.S. bachelor's equivalent.]

Equivalence through experience would require a separate evaluation and a qualifying position, and the work experience is described only as 'plus work experience' with no detail (p. 1). [Attorney: not shown on p. 1.] [Attorney: Obtain experience letters and consider an evaluation of education and experience combined, noting that the position itself remains the first problem.]

### Requirement 3: Cap exemption or cap registration
Governing rule: INA 214(g)(5) exempts certain petitioners from the numerical cap, including institutions of higher education, related or affiliated nonprofit entities, and nonprofit or governmental research organizations. Concurrent employment and certain prior cap-counted H-1B holders are also treated as exempt under the regulations. [Attorney: Confirm the precise regulatory citations before use.]

Assessment: Not met on the present file. The employer states that the filing is cap-exempt but gives no basis (p. 1). The employer is described as a physician's practice (p. 1). Nothing in the file shows it is an institution of higher education, an affiliated nonprofit, or a research organization, and no concurrent employment or prior cap-counted status is stated (p. 1). Absent a basis, the petition would be cap-subject and would require a registration selection. [Attorney: Ask the employer for the factual basis it relies on, including its organization type and tax status.]

### Requirement 4: Labor Condition Application and employer information
Governing rule: 8 CFR 214.2(h)(4)(i)(B) requires a certified Labor Condition Application before filing Form I-129.

Assessment: Not met. The file contains no Labor Condition Application, no offered wage, no proposed start date, and no employer name or EIN (p. 1). Without a job description, the occupational classification and wage level for the LCA cannot be determined.

The file also contains no H-1B registration or prior cap-counted H-1B history (p. 1). [Attorney: Obtain these facts at the next contact.]

### Overall view and next steps
On the present file, the petition cannot be approved. The position is not shown to be a specialty occupation, the worker's credential is below a bachelor's degree on the face of the file, and the cap exemption has no stated basis (p. 1). Each of the three defects is independent, so correcting one would not suffice. The prospects of approval as presented are poor.

Recommended steps, subject to the attorney's decision: (1) advise the employer before any filing that its stated legal theory appears unsound; (2) request the missing facts and documents listed above; (3) obtain a detailed job description and a credential evaluation to test whether any version of the position and the worker's qualifications could meet the standard; (4) ask for the factual basis of the claimed cap exemption; (5) consider whether another visa category could fit the worker and the employer.
