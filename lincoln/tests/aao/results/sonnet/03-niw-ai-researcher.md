### 3. Prong 1: Substantial Merit and National Importance of the Proposed Endeavor
Merit: The petitioner describes his work as developing medical AI approaches to model hidden early-stage disease risks from large-scale biomedical data using deep learning, CNN, LSTM, Transformer and transfer learning methods (p. 1). He states that he serves various companies and collaborates with hospitals on medical analytics and risk assessment (p. 1). Medical AI for early disease risk prediction plausibly has substantial merit, but the summary gives no documentary support.

Endeavor definition: The proposed endeavor is stated as applying AI, cloud architectures and biomedical big data toward 'the creation of state-of-the-art disease risk prediction tools' (p. 1). The planned topics are AI analysis of medical records, brain wave analysis and gene sequence function (p. 1). The summary gives no specific scope, deliverables, timeline, population, or disease focus for the US work. The petitioner also cites experience with national health data of millions of patients covering rheumatoid arthritis, type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease (p. 1). The planned topics are not tied to that experience or to any plan (p. 1).

Framing conflict to resolve: The petitioner intends to work as a senior researcher for the US office of his Taiwanese company (p. 1). He also describes his work as serving various companies and collaborating with hospitals (p. 1). These are two different framings of the endeavor. [Attorney: decide which framing applies. An employer-specific role and a broader independent research endeavor are argued differently.]

National importance: The petitioner argues that the endeavor would reduce US healthcare costs through prevention and early treatment (p. 1). The file also contains an article on hepatitis B, a CDC article on eliminating hepatitis B, a commentary on the impact of COVID-19 on the US, and references to national initiatives including ARPA-H (p. 1). None of these is described as relating to his own proposed work. The summary shows no evidence of the endeavor's prospective impact beyond general cost savings (p. 1). Under Dhanasar, national importance turns on the endeavor's potential prospective impact, not on the general importance of the field.

Assessment: substantial merit is plausible; national importance is not shown. The third-party general articles may dilute the petition. [Attorney: decide whether to narrow the endeavor to a specific, defined research program. Decide whether to drop or reframe the unrelated hepatitis B, CDC and COVID-19 materials, and whether to obtain evidence tying ARPA-H or similar initiatives directly to the proposed work.]
