### EB-1A Eligibility Assessment: Producer and Creative Director (Armenia) (for_review)
### 1. Scope and basis of this assessment
This assessment covers a prospective self-petition on Form I-140 for a television producer and creative director from Armenia under the extraordinary ability classification. It is based solely on a one-page summary; no underlying documents were received (p. 1).

No major internationally recognized award is claimed (p. 1). The petition therefore depends on meeting at least three of the regulatory criteria, followed by a final merits determination of sustained acclaim. The governing rule is 8 CFR 204.5(h)(3).

The summary claims five criteria: published material, judging, original contributions, leading or critical role, and high salary (p. 1). Each is addressed below. [Attorney: every fact below is drawn from the summary page and the intake notes; none is verified by a source document.]

### 2. Criterion 1: Published material about the beneficiary in major media
The file claims an extensive interview on bravo.am, supported by screenshots showing title and date (p. 1). The summary states that the page names the site but no individual author (p. 1). The screenshots themselves are not in the file.

To show the standing of the site, the file offers a page from together.nbcuni.com stating that Bravo is part of NBC Universal, a page from mediamax.am stating that bravo.am 'became the most popular entertainment web resource in Armenia', and the bravo.am About page stating that it is 'the most rated in the entertainment industry' with '500,000 monthly visits' (p. 1). The mediamax.am and About page statements are claims by those publishers and are not independently corroborated in the file. The NBC Universal page concerns a US television network owner and, as summarized, does not establish bravo.am's readership (p. 1). [Attorney: confirm whether the NBC Universal page relates to the Armenian website at all.]

The quoted passage, 'For me, the goal that my work serves is primary', is in the first person and suggests an interview of self-promotional tone rather than independent coverage of his work (p. 1). [Attorney: not shown on p. 1.] Only one item is claimed. The criterion requires published material about the person relating to his work in the field, with title, date and author shown, and evidence of major media status.

Assessment: not met on this file. The author is missing, the standing of the outlet rests on its own and one other Armenian statement, and the item is a single interview. [Attorney: obtain the full interview with date, any translation with certification, and independent circulation or audience data, and look for additional independent coverage.]

### 3. Criterion 2: Participation as a judge of the work of others
The file states that he was chairman of the selection committee of a national song contest, described as a singing competition (p. 1). No document shows his appointment, the contest's scope, how entrants were evaluated, or that he judged the work of others in the field of his claimed ability, which is television production and creative direction (p. 1).

Assessment: not met on this file; this is the only criterion with a plausible basis, and it is unproven. A singing competition may also be a different field from television production, which the attorney should weigh. [Attorney: obtain an appointment letter, contest rules, programs, broadcast records and a letter from the organizer, and assess whether the judging was in his field.]

### 4. Criterion 3: Original contributions of major significance
The file includes a letter from the executive director of the television company where he worked. The executive director states that his 'organizational and work planning methods as well as production knowledge contributed to more accurate and effective distribution of human and technical resources' (p. 1). This is the executive director's own statement and is not corroborated elsewhere in the file. The language is generic and does not show an impact on the field beyond that employer. The author is also an interested party. [Attorney: the letter itself is not in the file; obtain it, and consider independent expert letters and evidence of adoption of his methods elsewhere.]

The file also states that in the bravo.am interview he says parliament adopted a law in 2019 establishing a commemorative day after his initiative, but the parliament.am screenshots on file concern a law that does not mention that day (p. 1). The claim is therefore unsupported and the screenshots do not corroborate it. [Attorney: raise with the client whether other documents exist; do not rely on this claim until it is confirmed.]

Assessment: not met on this file. The evidence does not show originality or major significance in the field.

### 5. Criterion 4: Leading or critical role for distinguished organizations
The summary states only that he held senior producer roles at television companies (p. 1). There are no organization charts, employer letters on the distinction of the companies, or records of his duties and position (p. 1).

The summary also states that he produced the broadcasts of national events (p. 1). No credit list, broadcast record or third-party confirmation is in the file, and the statement is not tied to any claimed criterion. It could support this criterion if documented. [Attorney: obtain employment records, charts, broadcast credits and evidence of the reputation of each company.]

Assessment: not met on this file; the claim is not yet shown.

### 6. Criterion 5: High salary or remuneration
The summary refers to 'salary evidence compared with others in the field' (p. 1). No pay records, contracts, tax documents or comparative wage data are in the file, so neither his remuneration nor the comparison can be assessed (p. 1).

Assessment: not met on this file. [Attorney: obtain pay records for each role and reliable comparative data for television producers in Armenia or the relevant market, with currency and period stated.]

### 7. Threshold, final merits and intent to work in the United States
Three criteria are required under 8 CFR 204.5(h)(3). On this file none is established; judging has a plausible basis but is unproven (p. 1). [Attorney: not shown on p. 1.] Even if three criteria were met, a final merits determination of sustained national or international acclaim would follow.

An EB-1A petitioner must also show clear evidence of coming to continue work in the area of extraordinary ability (8 CFR 204.5(h)(5)). The file contains no plans, offers, contracts or letters of interest for work in the United States in television production or creative direction. [Attorney: obtain this evidence at intake.]
