### 2. Governing standard
An O-1A beneficiary must show extraordinary ability by sustained national or international acclaim, shown either by a major internationally recognized award or by evidence meeting at least three of the regulatory criteria. The governing rule is 8 CFR 214.2(o)(3)(iii). No major award is claimed (p. 1), so eligibility depends on at least three criteria being met with evidence. Only three criteria are claimed (p. 1), so each must be satisfied for the petition to meet the evidentiary threshold on this file.

### 3. Criterion: critical or essential capacity for distinguished organizations
The claim rests on the beneficiary's role leading export sales at the company (p. 1). The file does not show that the company has a distinguished reputation, and it contains no organizational chart, company letter or other evidence that his role is critical or essential to the company. The criterion is not shown to be met on the present file.

[Attorney: Obtain evidence of the organization's distinguished reputation, the org chart, a detailed letter from the company describing his role and its importance, and evidence of his contribution to export results.]

### 4. Criterion: high salary or remuneration
The page states that salary evidence compared with others in the field is claimed (p. 1). No salary figure, offer terms, pay records or comparative wage data for export sales directors appear in the file (p. 1). The criterion cannot be assessed and is not shown to be met.

[Attorney: Obtain the employment contract, pay records, and reliable comparative wage data for similar positions, including any data for the Finnish parent if relevant.]

### 5. Criterion: published material about the beneficiary
The firm cites coverage from reuters.com, dwell.com, archdaily.com, markets.ft.com, honka.com and kauppalehti.fi about the company's homes, exports and business results, and states that the coverage shows consistent third-party reporting on his work across multiple internationally recognized platforms (p. 1). The page also states that none of the articles names him (p. 1).

The regulatory criterion requires published material about the beneficiary relating to his work in the field. Articles that do not name or discuss him are about the company, not about him, and do not meet the criterion. The firm's characterization of the coverage as reporting on his work is not supported by the page (p. 1). The actual articles are not in the file.

Further, honka.com appears to be the manufacturer's own site, although the page does not say so. If so, it is not major media or a professional or major trade publication and cannot count even if it named him. [Attorney: Confirm ownership of honka.com and review the actual articles.] This criterion is not met on the present file.
