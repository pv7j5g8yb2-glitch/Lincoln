### Eligibility Assessment: Form I-360 VAWA Self-Petition (Abused Spouse of a US Citizen), Initial Consultation, Rivera Immigration Law (for_review)
### 1. Scope and basis of the assessment
Prepared for Alex Rivera. The file is a one-page summary of the client's account and of four documents. It is not the documents themselves. The findings below weigh the evidence as described, under the preponderance standard (Matter of Chawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369 (AAO 2010)). The sender's name is not given and no identifiers (full name, A-Number, dates of birth, addresses, the child's details) are in the file. The page is legible and no part of it is unreadable.

The client is a Mexican woman who states that she last entered the United States in November 2001 and married a US citizen, identified as T-D-, in Indiana in 2002. (p. 1) She wishes to file Form I-360 as an abused spouse, claiming battery and extreme cruelty. (p. 1) She states that this is her only marriage and that she is still married to him, although they have not lived together since 2009. (p. 1)

The governing provisions are INA 204(a)(1)(A)(iii) and 8 CFR 204.2(c). The self-petitioner must show, among other things, a qualifying marriage to a US citizen, a good faith marriage, residence with the abuser at some time, battery or extreme cruelty during the marriage, and good moral character. Under INA 204(a)(1)(J), USCIS must consider any credible evidence relevant to the petition.

### 2. Requirement: legally valid marriage that subsists, or ended by divorce within the permitted period
Finding: likely met, subject to resolving the docket discrepancy.

The file lists a marriage certificate from Indiana dated 2002. (p. 1) The 2009 Verified Petition for Support and Medical Insurance states that the parties married in Indiana in 2002 and 'are not divorced nor is there pending any legal separation or dissolution proceeding'. (p. 1) The 2010 Agreed Judgment is described as dealing only with the child's financial care. (p. 1) These documents point to a subsisting marriage, and the file contains no decree of dissolution.

The countervailing item is a USCIS reading of the 2009 court docket. It described the case as a divorce filing 'Disposed of in Error'. (p. 1) The same 2009 case is described as a domestic relations case brought by the client for custody, child support and medical insurance, marked 'disposed' in 2010. (p. 1) The file does not say what USCIS or when, and no USCIS notice is in the file. The reading is a secondhand description of a docket entry. It is outweighed by the sworn petition and by the description of the judgment, neither of which refers to a dissolution. On this record, a preponderance favors a subsisting marriage.

This requirement is outcome-determinative. If a dissolution decree exists, she could no longer rely on the marriage. A divorce in or about 2010 would appear to fall outside the two-year period after divorce that INA 204(a)(1)(A)(iii) allows, and the petition could not succeed. Good faith, abuse and good moral character would then not be reached. The client's statement that this is her only marriage is self reported and is not corroborated in the file. A prior undissolved marriage by either spouse would affect validity.

The file states that the husband is a US citizen. (p. 1) No proof of his citizenship is in the file. The statement is unopposed, but the petition requires evidence of it, such as a birth certificate, passport, naturalization certificate or other proof. ### 4. Requirement: marriage entered in good faith
Finding: not met on the present record.

The file lists good faith marriage among the evidence not yet gathered. (p. 1) The marriage certificate and the 2009 pleading show that the marriage occurred and that the couple have a child. (p. 1) They do not show the client's intent at the time of marriage. The client's own statement is the only other source, and it is not in the file. The finding is unfavorable only because the record is empty, and the element is ordinarily provable with a declaration, joint records, photographs, and the couple's history. [Attorney: determine what joint evidence the client holds, given that the couple have lived apart since 2009. (p. 1)]

### 5. Requirement: residence with the abusive spouse
Finding: not met on the present record; likely achievable.

The file states that the couple have not lived together since 2009 and lists shared residence as evidence not yet gathered. (p. 1) It does not state where or for how long the couple lived together before 2009, or whether any shared residence was in the United States. The statute requires that she have resided with the spouse at some time. The marriage in 2002 and the separation in 2009 suggest a period of cohabitation, but the file does not establish it. [Attorney: obtain residence history, leases, and joint records for 2002 to 2009.]

### 6. Requirement: battery or extreme cruelty during the marriage
Finding: not met on the present record.

The client claims battery and extreme cruelty. (p. 1) No evidence of the abuse has been gathered. (p. 1) The file does not describe any incident, date, or location, and it does not say whether the abuse occurred before the 2009 separation. The documents described in the file are the 2009 petition and the 2010 judgment, both about custody and support. (p. 1) The file's description of them does not mention abuse. The standard is any credible evidence under INA 204(a)(1)(J), and the definition at 8 CFR 204.2(c)(1)(vi) covers acts of psychological or sexual abuse as well as physical violence. The client's declaration, police or medical records, protective orders and statements from third parties could suffice. However, the attorney should expect USCIS to ask how the claim is reconciled with a 2009 support filing that, as described, raises no abuse. [Attorney: interview the client on the abuse timeline and on why it was not raised in 2009.]

### 7. Requirement: good moral character
Finding: cannot be determined; no adverse fact is in the file.

The file lists good moral character evidence as not yet gathered. (p. 1) It states nothing about arrests, convictions, prior removal or refusal, unauthorized work, or any false claim to US citizenship. The relevant standard is INA 101(f) and 8 CFR 204.2(c)(1)(vii). Typical evidence is the client's affidavit with local police clearances for the places she has lived in the last three years before filing. [Attorney: ask the full history at the consultation before any filing decision.]

### 8. Other matters: residence, entry, and later adjustment
The client states that she last entered in November 2001. (p. 1) The file does not say whether she was inspected and admitted or paroled, and it does not say whether she had earlier entries or whether she has lived in the United States continuously. Manner of entry is not an element of the I-360 itself. It matters for any later Form I-485 under INA 245(a), which requires inspection and admission or parole, and for inadmissibility questions arising from unlawful presence or prior entries. [Attorney: obtain the entry documents, and any I-94 or visa, and consider adjustment eligibility before filing.]

The reference to a USCIS reading of the docket (p. 1) suggests contact with USCIS, but the file has no receipt, notice or decision. [Attorney: ask the client whether any earlier petition, application, request for evidence or denial exists. Any earlier filing is history and would not bind USCIS here, but it may affect strategy.]

(p. 1) The 2010 judgment is described as dealing only with the child's financial care. (p. 1) The documents point to different pictures and the discrepancy must be resolved from certified records.

Gap: the 2009 filing was a support action, brought by the client against T-D-, in which, as described, no abuse is mentioned. (p. 1) The couple have lived apart since 2009. (p. 1) The client has not yet gathered evidence of abuse, good faith marriage, shared residence or good moral character. (p. 1)

### 11. Overall view and next steps
Prospects cannot yet be rated favorable. The threshold element, a subsisting marriage, is likely met on the documents as described, and nothing credible in the file establishes a divorce. Every other element is presently unsupported rather than contradicted. If certified records show a dissolution decree, the petition fails at the first element and the later steps are not reached. If they confirm the marriage, the outcome depends on whether credible evidence of abuse and good faith can be assembled, and on the client's history.
