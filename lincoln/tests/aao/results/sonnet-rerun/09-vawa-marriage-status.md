### Admissibility and Later Adjustment of Status
The self-petition itself does not require admissibility, but later adjustment does. The summary states that the Applicant last entered in November 2001 (p. 1). It does not state the manner of entry, any inspection or admission, any status, or any earlier entries and departures. The words 'last entered' imply earlier travel, and none is documented.

Under INA § 212(a)(9)(B), more than 180 days and less than one year of unlawful presence followed by departure triggers a three-year bar, and one year or more triggers a ten-year bar. The bar runs from the departure. An exception exists for battered spouses and children where there is a connection to the abuse. Under INA § 212(a)(9)(C), more than one year of aggregate unlawful presence, followed by entry or attempted entry without inspection, creates a permanent bar. The file does not allow any computation, because entry dates, departure dates and status are missing. If the Applicant entered without inspection, adjustment would depend on other routes and on the attorney's analysis of the VAWA exceptions. [Attorney: Please obtain the complete travel and entry history, including I-94 records, visas and any prior removals or applications, before advising on adjustment.]
