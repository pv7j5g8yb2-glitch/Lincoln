### Governing Standard
An O-1A beneficiary must show extraordinary ability in business through sustained national or international acclaim, and must show that he or she is coming to continue work in the area of extraordinary ability. INA § 101(a)(15)(O)(i). Absent a major internationally recognized award, the regulation requires evidence of at least three of the listed criteria, and USCIS then considers the totality of the evidence in a final merits determination. 8 C.F.R. § 214.2(o)(3)(iii). The Petitioner bears the burden of proof by a preponderance of the evidence. Matter of Chawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369 (AAO 2010). Eligibility must exist at filing and continue through adjudication. 8 C.F.R. § 103.2(b)(1). Statements in a brief or by the firm are not evidence, and approvals for other persons do not bind USCIS.
