### Requirement 1: Specialty Occupation
Finding: not met.

An H-1B position must be a specialty occupation, which requires at least a bachelor's degree or its equivalent in a specific specialty as a minimum for entry into the occupation. See INA § 214(i)(1); 8 C.F.R. § 214.2(h)(4)(ii). The Petitioner's own stated minimum is an associate level assistant nursing degree plus experience (p. 1). That requirement is below the bachelor's level, so the position fails the test on the Petitioner's own description.

The Petitioner's reliance on the listing of 'medicine and health' in 8 C.F.R. § 214.2(h)(4)(ii) (p. 1) is a statement of the employer's position, not evidence. The listing of a field does not make every job in the field a specialty occupation. The file contains no job description, duties, or evidence of industry practice that would show a degree requirement. The absence of any description beyond the title (p. 1) is itself a deficiency.

Because this requirement fails and the case cannot succeed without it, the later steps are not reached: the Beneficiary's qualifications under 8 C.F.R. § 214.2(h)(4)(iii)(C), the Labor Condition Application, the cap analysis and the Beneficiary's admissibility would not cure the petition.

### Requirement 2: Beneficiary's Qualifications and Credential Equivalence
Finding: not met.

The Beneficiary must hold a US bachelor's degree or a foreign degree determined to be equivalent, in the specialty. See 8 C.F.R. § 214.2(h)(4)(iii)(C). The Beneficiary holds a two year associate degree after secondary school (p. 1). The Petitioner states that there is no educational difference between an associate's and a bachelor's degree in Syria (p. 1). That statement is unsupported. No credential evaluation, expert opinion, transcript or certified translation is in the file. The credential is named only in transliteration (p. 1).

Equivalence through a combination of education and experience would require separate documented proof under the regulation. The file describes only 'work experience' (p. 1), without dates, employers or duties. ### Requirement 3: H-1B Cap Exemption
Finding: not met.

A petition is cap exempt only if the Petitioner is, among other categories, an institution of higher education, a nonprofit entity related to or affiliated with such an institution, or a nonprofit or governmental research organization. See INA § 214(g)(5). The Petitioner states that the filing is exempt and gives no basis (p. 1). A physician's practice is not shown in the file to fall within any exempt category. Without an exemption the petition is subject to the H-1B cap and the registration process. The file contains no registration, and the timing of any registration is not stated. [Attorney: confirm the Petitioner's entity type and any claimed affiliation.]

### Requirement 4: Beneficiary's Identity, Status and Admissibility
Finding: cannot be assessed.

The file gives no name, date or place of birth, passport, location, entry history, current status, prior filings or arrest history for the Beneficiary. Under the consultation rulebook, facts must be established and confirmed by records before any filing, and the bars and grounds, including unlawful presence, misrepresentation and criminal grounds, cannot be evaluated. The criminal consequences framework is not triggered by any fact stated, but the absence of arrest information leaves it unresolved.

### Evidentiary Standard and Unrepresented Petitioner
The burden rests on the Petitioner by a preponderance of the evidence. Matter of Chawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369 (AAO 2010). Eligibility must exist at the time of filing and through adjudication. 8 C.F.R. § 103.2(b)(1). Later evidence cannot cure a petition that was not approvable when filed. Assertions in a brief or letter are not evidence. The Petitioner intends to file without counsel on its own reading of the regulation (p. 1). A filing on these facts risks denial and a record that could affect the Beneficiary.
