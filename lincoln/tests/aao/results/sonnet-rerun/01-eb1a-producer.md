### Eligibility Assessment: Form I-140, EB-1A Extraordinary Ability (Television Producer and Creative Director, Armenia) (for_review)
### 1. Summary and Posture
The matter is at the initial consultation and eligibility review stage and is waiting on the attorney, Alex Rivera, for review. No petition has been prepared or filed. The file consists of a one-page summary of the claim. The summary describes evidence but does not contain it. The sender gave no name; the only identifier is the email address client@eval.example. The individual is referred to below as the Beneficiary, and the person filing the self-petition as the Petitioner; they are the same person in a self-petition.

The file describes a self-petition on Form I-140 under the extraordinary ability classification. The Beneficiary is a television producer and creative director from Armenia. No major internationally recognized award is claimed, and five criteria are claimed (p. 1). The Beneficiary must therefore satisfy at least three of the criteria listed at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3) and then satisfy the final merits determination. See INA § 203(b)(1)(A). The burden rests on the Petitioner by a preponderance of the evidence. Matter of Chawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369 (AAO 2010). Eligibility must exist at filing and continue through adjudication, and statements in a brief are not evidence.

Overall finding: the threshold of three criteria is not met on this file. Two criteria are arguable at most, and neither is documented. Details follow.

### 2. Criterion 1: Published Material About the Beneficiary in Major Media (8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iii))
The claimed evidence is an extensive interview on bravo.am, with screenshots showing title and date (p. 1). The page names the site but no individual author (p. 1). The Beneficiary quotes himself in the interview as saying 'For me, the goal that my work serves is primary.' (p. 1).

To show the standing of the site, the file lists a page from together.nbcuni.com stating that Bravo is part of NBC Universal and concerns US television networks (p. 1). It also lists a mediamax.am page stating that bravo.am 'became the most popular entertainment web resource in Armenia' (p. 1), and the bravo.am About page, which claims it is 'the most rated in the entertainment industry' with '500,000 monthly visits' (p. 1).

Finding: not met. The regulation requires published material about the beneficiary and his work, in professional or major trade publications or other major media, with title, date and author. An interview in which the Beneficiary speaks about his own views is of doubtful value as material about him, and no author is identified. The standing of the site rests on its own About page claim, which is self-promotional, and on a mediamax.am statement that is a single outside assertion without traffic or circulation data. The nbcuni.com page concerns US networks, and the file does not show that bravo.am is the same entity or is related to the Bravo described there. The 'primary' quotation appears unrelated to the criterion.

[Attorney: decide whether to obtain independent circulation or audience data for bravo.am, a certified translation of the interview, and any articles by named authors about the Beneficiary. Decide whether this criterion should be claimed at all.]

### 3. Criterion 2: Participation as a Judge of the Work of Others (8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iv))
The file states that the Beneficiary was chairman of the selection committee of a national song contest, described as a singing competition (p. 1).

Finding: not met on this file, though the criterion is arguable. The regulation requires participation, individually or on a panel, as a judge of the work of others in the same or an allied field of specialization. No appointment letter, contest rules, committee records, or evidence of the contest's national standing is in the file. The contest is a singing competition, while the claimed field is television production and creative direction. Whether judging singers is the same or an allied field is a question the file does not answer. Participation as chairman of a selection committee may also differ from judging the work of others, and the file does not state what the committee decided.

[Attorney: obtain proof of appointment, the contest's rules and broadcast or organizer records, and evidence of what the committee evaluated. Decide how to argue the allied field point.]

### 4. Criterion 3: Original Contributions of Major Significance (8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(v))
The file contains a letter from the executive director of the television company where the Beneficiary worked. The executive director states that the Beneficiary's 'organizational and work planning methods as well as production knowledge contributed to more accurate and effective distribution of human and technical resources' (p. 1). The Beneficiary produced the broadcasts of national events (p. 1). In the bravo.am interview, the Beneficiary says that parliament adopted a law in 2019 establishing a commemorative day after his initiative (p. 1). The file has parliament.am screenshots about a law that does not mention that day (p. 1).

Finding: not met. The letter is from a former employer, an interested party, and describes internal methods in general terms. It does not show that the contribution was of major significance in the field or was adopted beyond that company. The statement that producing national event broadcasts is a contribution does not show originality or significance. The claim about the 2019 law is uncorroborated and is contradicted by the only official source in the file, which does not mention the day. The inconsistency between the interview and the parliament.am pages must be resolved before the claim is used. Relying on it unresolved risks the credibility of the whole petition.

[Attorney: ask the Beneficiary to explain the 2019 law claim and obtain the correct law text or other official record. Obtain independent letters from experts or industry figures who do not have an employment relationship with him, and evidence of adoption or impact of his methods.]

### 5. Criterion 4: Leading or Critical Role (8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(viii))
The file states only that the Beneficiary held senior producer roles at television companies (p. 1). The companies are not named.

Finding: not met. No organizational charts, employment letters, contracts or evidence of the distinguished reputation of the organizations are in the file. The criterion requires a leading or critical role for organizations with a distinguished reputation, and neither the role nor the reputation is documented.

[Attorney: obtain the names of the companies, role descriptions, organizational charts, and evidence of each organization's reputation.]

### 6. Criterion 5: High Salary or Remuneration (8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(ix))
The file states only that the claim is 'salary evidence compared with others in the field' (p. 1). No salary figure, employer, pay record or comparative data is in the file.

Finding: not met. The criterion requires evidence of a high salary in relation to others in the field, and nothing in the file establishes either the Beneficiary's remuneration or the comparison.

[Attorney: obtain contracts, pay records or tax documents, and reliable comparative salary data for producers in Armenia and the relevant market.]

### 7. Threshold and Final Merits Determination
At most criteria 2 and 3 are arguable, and neither is documented, so the requirement of three criteria is not met. The case cannot succeed without it, and the second step, the final merits determination, is therefore not reached on this file. Even if three criteria were met, USCIS would then assess whether the totality of the evidence shows sustained national or international acclaim and that the Beneficiary is among the small percentage at the top of the field. No evidence of acclaim is in the file. See Kazarian v. USCIS, 596 F.3d 1115 (9th Cir. 2010). The file also contains nothing showing that the Beneficiary intends to continue work in his area of expertise in the United States, which the regulation requires at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(5).

### 8. Facts-First Review and Other Items
The file does not contain the Beneficiary's name, passport, entries and exits, status history, prior applications, arrests or court cases, family members, or employment history. The bars and grounds review cannot be performed, and no advice on timing or risks can be given until these are collected and confirmed by records requests. Nothing in the file indicates a criminal matter, and the criminal consequences analysis is not reached on the present facts.

### 9. Overall View and Next Steps
Prospects on the present file are poor. No criterion is met, the evidence is described rather than supplied, and one claim in the Beneficiary's interview is contradicted by an official source in the file. A petition filed on this record would likely draw a Request for Evidence (RFE) or a denial.

Recommended next steps, for attorney decision: [Attorney: (1) obtain the Beneficiary's name, identity documents and full immigration, criminal and family history, and consider Freedom of Information Act requests where there is any prior US contact; (2) obtain the originals of all exhibits described on page 1, namely the bravo.am interview and translation, the executive director letter, the contest committee records, employment and role evidence, salary data and the parliament.am pages; (3) resolve the 2019 law inconsistency before any use of the interview; (4) obtain independent expert letters; (5) decide whether to advise the client that EB-2 under Matter of Dhanasar, 26 I&N Dec. 884 (AAO 2016), or O-1 classification should be evaluated as alternatives; (6) send written advice to the client only after attorney review.]
