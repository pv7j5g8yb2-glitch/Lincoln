Appeal of U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) Decision

Form I-129, Petition for a Nonimmigrant Worker (H-lB)



The Petitioner seeks to temporarily employ the Beneficiary under the H-lB nonimmigrant classification
for specialty occupations. See Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act) section 101(a)(15)(H)(i)(b),
8 U.S.C. § 1101(a)(15)(H)(i)(b). The H-lB program allows a U.S. employer to file a petition to
temporarily employ a qualified foreign worker in an occupation that requires both: (a) the theoretical
and practical application of a body of highly specialized knowledge; and (b) the attainment of a
bachelor's or higher degree in the specific specialty (or its equivalent) as a minimum prerequisite for
entry into the occupation.

Through its Service Center Operations (SCOPS), USCIS denied the Form I-129, Petition for a
Nonimmigrant Worker (petition), concluding the record did not establish that the filing was exempt
from the H-lB numerical limitations cap, nor did it demonstrate that the position was a specialty
occupation. The matter is now before us on appeal under 8 C.F.R. § 103.3. The Petitioner bears the
burden ofproof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. Section 291 of the Act;
Matter of Chawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375 (AAO 2010). We review the questions in this matter de
novo. Matter of Christo 's Inc., 26 I&N Dec. 537, 537 n.2 (AAO 2015). Upon de novo review, we
will dismiss the appeal.

We adopt and affirm SCOPS' decision. See Matter ofS-K-E-, 30 I&N Dec. 27, 29 (BIA 2026) (citing
Matter ofBurbano, 20 I&N Dec. 872, 874 (BIA 1994); see also see also Garland v. Ming Dai, 593
U.S. 357, 370-71 (2021) (noting an administrative appellate body's act of adopting and affirming the
below decision is legitimate, incorporating the lower decision's analysis and reasoning on the issues
into the appellate determination).

We note that the Petitioner indicated on the Form I-290B, Notice of Appeal or Motion, that it would
provide a brief and/or additional evidence within 30 days of filing the appeal. However, we have not
received a follow-on brief within the allotted timeframe or thereafter.

The petition filing itself and the arguments offered in the appeal reveal the petitioning organization either
does not possess the requisite familiarity with H-lB requirements or it received inaccurate advice, even
though they have not been represented by an immigration attorney throughout these proceedings.
The Petitioner filed this petition for the position of a nursing assistant. First, we note that USCIS policy
states that "[r]egistered nurses generally do not qualify for H-lB classification. This is because most
[registered nurse] positions do not normally require a U.S. bachelor's or higher degree in nursing (or its
equivalent) as the minimum for entry into these particular positions and thus are unable to qualify as
specialty occupations." See generally USCIS Policy Memorandum PM-602-0104, Adjudication ofH-JB
Petitions for Nursing Occupations 2 (Feb. 18, 2015), http://www.uscis.gov/legal-resources/policy­
memoranda. This policy memorandum recognizes that "having a degree is not, by itself, sufficient for
the position to qualify for H-lB classification. A critical factor remains whether a baccalaureate or higher
degree in a specific specialty (or its equivalent) is normally required for these particular positions." Id. at
5.

That same memorandum contains a caveat for some nursing occupations that might qualify based on
increased requirements:

        Advanced practice registered nurse defines a level of nursing practice that utilizes
        extended and expanded skills, experience and knowledge in assessment, planning,
        implementation, diagnosis and evaluation of the care required. Positions that require
        nurses who are certified [advanced practice registered nurses] will generally be specialty
        occupations due to the advanced level of education and training required for certification.

Id. at 4-5. That same policy memorandum offers a non-exhaustive list of advanced practice registered
nursing occupations that may satisfy the specialty occupation requirements to include: certified
nurse-midwife; certified clinical nurse specialist; certified nurse practitioner; and certified registered
nurse anesthetist. Id. at 5. Notably, each of those occupations are included in DOL's Occupational
Outlook Handbook or corresponding Occupational Information Network (O*NET) entries as typically
requiring a master's degree in an advanced practice nursing field. Bureau of Labor Statistics, U.S. Dep't
ofLabor, Occupational Outlook Handbook, Nurse Anesthetists, Nurse Midwives, and Nurse Practitioners
(Aug. 28, 2025), https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/nurse-anesthetists-nurse-midwives-and-nurse­
practitioners.htrn#tab-4.

But the position in this petition is not within the categories of some nursing occupations listed in that
policy memorandum that "may qualify as specialty occupations." See generally USCIS Policy
Memorandum PM-602-0104, supra at 4. As a final note about this memorandum, it provides that each
individual state's requirements to obtain a nursing license also factor into whether a position satisfies the
specialty occupation definition. Id. at 5.

Beyond that, the Petitioner offered no correspondence in the initial filing to inform USCIS of the specific
work the Beneficiary would perform or what the organization mandates as the minimum education
requirements for the position. It wasn't until SCOPS issued a notice informing the Petitioner that the
failure to provide a wide array of documents and information would result in the petition's denial that the
organization provided a letter indicating the position's minimum requirements consisted of "anAssistant
Nursing degree and demonstrated professional experience in the specialty area." That is far short of this
nonimmigrant classification's requirements of a theoretical and practical application of a body of highly
specialized knowledge, and attainment of a bachelor's or higher degree in the specific specialty (or its
equivalent) as a minimum for entry into the occupation in the United States. Section 214(i)(l) of the Act,

Additionally, the arguments attached to the Form I-290B continue to reflect a lack of familiarity, or
erroneous guidance from an outside source. Below we note some ofthe incorrect information in the short
statement attached to the appeal:

        Specialty Occupation - 8 CFR 214.2(h)(4)(ii) identifies "medicine and health" as a field
        of specialized knowledge, of which nursing falls under, and the physician is in need of
        such specialized knowledge to provide the appropriate care to patients, as such, further
        information will be provided in the supporting brief.

While the actual text of "medicine and health" may be included in the regulatory definition of a specialty
occupation, not every occupation operating in medicine and health is included as a qualifying one.
Emergency medical technicians and paramedics also operate in the medicine and health field, but are
similarly not qualifying for the H-1 B program.

        Qualifying Criteria I - The degree is normally the m1mmum requirement for the
        occupation as there is no educational difference between an Associate's and a Bachelor's
        degree in the country of Syria, thus this degree is the minimum requirement in that
        country, and as such, further information will be provided in the supporting brief.

        Qualifying Criteria II - The degree is normally the minimum requirement for the
        occupation as there is no educational difference between an Associate's and a Bachelor's
        degree in the country of Syria required to perform the job duties in parallel positions
        among similar organizations in the United States, as such, further information will be
        provided in the supporting brief.

The statement that "there is no educational difference between an Associate's and a Bachelor's degree in
the country of Syria" is false. According to the American Association of Collegiate Registrars and
Admissions Officers Electronic Database for Global Education (EDGE), 1 in Syria the relevant
education levels for this case are:

     1. One of two technical Baccalaureates; either a Al-Shahada Al Thanawiyya-Al'Amma or a
        Shahada Al Thanawiyya Al-Fanniyya). Both are awarded upon completion of 12 years of
        primary, intermediate, and secondary education and represents attainment of a level of
        education comparable to completion of a vocational or other specialized high school
        curriculum in the United States.
     2. The above baccalaureates have a path to a Wathiqat-Musaddiqat Takarruj (associate degree)
        or a Certificate of Licensed Assistant requiring two years of study.
