# Case: U visa, attempted armed robbery at a restaurant (Mexican man)

A Mexican man filed Form I-918 for U-1 status in 2017; USCIS asked for more evidence. The firm now has the file.

* Supplement B (signed by the police department's Records Coordinator): victim of felonious assault and attempted
  robbery; a second Supplement B cites California Penal Code 211 (robbery) and 245(a) (assault with a deadly weapon):
  two masked men entered the restaurant where he worked, assaulted him and other employees with a gun and demanded money.
  Injury section: he "was extremely frightened throughout the assault."
* Police report (2012): attempted robbery; early morning, he, his wife and son were preparing to open; two masked men
  pointed a gun at his son and demanded the safe be opened; video shows one man with a bag, one with a gun, the
  petitioner and son on the floor with hands up; the men left about a minute and a half after entering. He told police
  the men "did not strike them but did push them to move them around." No injuries recorded.
* His 2017 statement: both men had guns, one pointed at his son, one at him; they were pushed together; the men left when
  no one could open the safe; he suffered emotional trauma, saw a doctor and a psychologist, took prescription
  medication for a few months. His later statement: a gun was pointed at his head, he thought they would all die; he
  became afraid to go out and feared strangers; still feels the effects.
* Son's statement: he became solitary, stopped taking grandchildren to the park, attended therapy until workers'
  compensation stopped paying.
* Urgent care, 12 days after: anxiety, insomnia, palpitations several times a day; PTSD diagnosed, anti anxiety
  medication, referral to a psychologist, no work restrictions; nine days later symptoms several times a week. Workers'
  compensation form: able to do his usual work without restrictions.
* Psychologist, November 2012 and a 2016 letter: four sessions; after the robbers left "without harming the family"
  they calmed down and continued their scheduled shift; adjustment disorder with anxiety; he copes "relatively well",
  "anxious in an expected fashion", keeps up enjoyable activities, "somewhat worried" for family.
* New psychologist, April 2025: he now says the men hit him, his wife and son with the pistol and threatened to kill
  them; many symptoms (flashbacks, night terrors, avoidance); PTSD, moderate, chronic; his checklist score is higher than
  his own account suggests; he says he has never taken psychiatric medication; he and his wife now run a successful
  restaurant; his biggest problems are his wife's health and his immigration status; he copes with walks and talking.
* His son received U status for the same incident.

Task: assess whether he can show substantial physical or mental abuse, and any other problems in the file.
