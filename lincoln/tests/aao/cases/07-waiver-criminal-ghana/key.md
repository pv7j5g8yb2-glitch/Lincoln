# Key: USCIS AAO, 6 Oct 2026 (In Re: 42610203), appeal DISMISSED

Source: pdf/07-waiver-criminal.pdf.

Required findings:
1. Both waivers turn on extreme hardship to qualifying relatives (212(h) allows spouse, parent, son or daughter; 212(i)
   allows only a spouse or parent, so the son does not count for 212(i)). The 15 year rehabilitation route under
   212(h)(1)(A) is not available.
2. With no sworn statement of intent from the wife, hardship must be shown in BOTH separation and relocation.
3. Separation hardship NOT extreme: nearby parents and sisters already help and nothing shows they cannot help more; no
   showing professional care is unavailable; incomes of about $3,000 (his) and $3,655 (hers) show some difficulty but
   not extreme financial hardship; the medical conditions (ear tubes, back pain) are not shown to exceed the usual
   consequences of removal, even in the aggregate (Matter of Pilch, Matter of Ige).
4. Because separation fails, the requirement fails regardless of relocation; discretion is not reached.
5. Assertions need supporting evidence (Policy Manual 9 B.6(B)).

Bad failures: finding extreme hardship on this record; counting the son as a qualifying relative for 212(i); ignoring the
need to address both scenarios.
