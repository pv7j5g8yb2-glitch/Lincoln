# Case: I-601 waiver for two crimes involving moral turpitude and misrepresentation (212(h) and 212(i)), Ghanaian husband

A Ghanaian man is inadmissible under INA 212(a)(2)(A) for two convictions for crimes involving moral turpitude and under
212(a)(6)(C)(i) for misrepresenting his criminal history; he does not contest either finding. He seeks waivers under
212(h) and 212(i). Qualifying relatives: his US citizen wife (married 2024) and US citizen son (born 2020). The
convictions are not more than 15 years old. His wife has not stated whether she would stay in the US or move to Ghana.

Evidence:
* His statement: his presence is essential to the family's finances, health and emotions; the son has a chronic ear
  condition that needed tubes in both ears; the wife has debilitating back problems; his daily caregiving is vital.
* Wife's affidavit: he owns a tree care and removal business (about $3,000 a month); she is a mechanical engineer
  (about $3,655 a month); together they cover the mortgage and debts; without him she would fall behind, damage her
  credit, need a second job and costly childcare, or face foreclosure. Her retired parents live nearby and do school
  runs and after school care but are getting older. She has chronic back pain and sciatica, chiropractic care, and
  relies on him for caregiving and housework. She has strong ties to family, community, church and friends.
* Support letters from her parents and sisters describing close relationships and frequent time together.
* Tax, employment, financial, medical records; photos; articles on Ghana, credit scores, childcare, the medical
  conditions, and parental absence.

Task: assess the waivers.
