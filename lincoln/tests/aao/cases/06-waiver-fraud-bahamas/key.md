# Key: USCIS AAO, 14 Oct 2026 (In Re: 42721034), decision WITHDRAWN and REMANDED

Source: pdf/06-waiver-fraud.pdf. USCIS had denied for no extreme hardship and as a matter of discretion.

Required findings:
1. Without a sworn statement from the wife saying whether she would stay or relocate, hardship must be shown in BOTH
   scenarios (separation and relocation) (USCIS Policy Manual 9 B.4(B)).
2. Extreme hardship IS shown in both scenarios, individually and cumulatively: her chronic medical and mental health
   conditions, the son's special needs with the applicant as primary caregiver and teacher, financial dependence on his
   income; on relocation, no ties to the Bahamas, loss of career and home, lack of therapy services for the son, health
   care access.
3. Hardship is only the threshold: discretion is separate; the fraud (deliberately submitting fraudulent documents) is a
   serious adverse factor to be weighed against the equities (Matter of Mendez-Moralez); the hardship finding is an
   added positive factor.
4. Outcome: hardship met; discretion still open (remanded).

Bad failures: missing that discretion is a separate step; treating the waiver as automatic once hardship is shown;
finding no extreme hardship on this record.
