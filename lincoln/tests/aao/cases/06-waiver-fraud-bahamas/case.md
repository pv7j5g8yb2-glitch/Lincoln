# Case: I-601 waiver for fraud or misrepresentation (212(i)), Bahamian husband

A Bahamian man applying to adjust status through his US citizen wife was found inadmissible under INA 212(a)(6)(C)(i)
for submitting fraudulent documents to obtain an immigration benefit; he does not contest the finding. He seeks a 212(i)
waiver. His wife is the only qualifying relative. She has not stated whether she would stay in the US or move to the
Bahamas if the waiver is denied.

Evidence:
* Wife's affidavit: high blood pressure and anxiety on medication; she relies on him to manage her stress, watch her
  blood pressure and care for her when unwell. Their son (born 2017) has autism and ADHD and needs constant supervision,
  speech therapy three times a week, occupational therapy twice a week and daily home schooling. She is a full time high
  school teacher; he home schools the boy Monday to Friday, takes him to therapy and works full time evenings and
  overnight. Without him she could not afford the home, the therapies or living costs and would have to cut hours or
  resign. If she moved: she was born and raised in the US, owns a home, has no ties to the Bahamas, would lose her
  career; the Bahamas lacks the specialized therapy and education the boy needs; poor healthcare access, few
  specialists, medication shortages, high costs.
* His affidavit: he is the boy's primary caregiver and teacher and contributes his full time income.
* February 2026 psychologist's evaluation: hypertension and generalized anxiety disorder, medicated, caused by his
  immigration situation; single parenting a special needs child while working full time would likely exceed her
  emotional capacity. Treating physician's letter: both chronic conditions needing ongoing management.
* Extensive medical and school records for the son (ASD, ADHD; home schooling, occupational, ABA, counseling, physical
  and speech therapy, often weekly), naming him as the instructor; proof of his employment and income.

Task: assess the waiver.
