# Case: T visa, labor trafficking of an H-2B hotel housekeeper (Filipino woman)

A Filipino woman wants to file Form I-914 for T-1 status (filing in 2019). She entered in December 2005, November 2007
and October 2009 as an H-2B hotel housekeeper, recruited by D-H- and the N-P- Agency.

Her account and evidence:
* Recruiters used false promises; her family took large loans against the family farm and land to pay recruitment fees.
* In the US she did not get the promised wages, benefits, hours or room and board; she could not repay the loans or pay
  her mother's medical bills. At orientation she was told she could be deported for breaking rules and was asked for
  her passport (she gave a copy). She was pushed to work more in less time and repeatedly threatened with arrest and
  deportation by the hotels, D-H- and the agency if she stopped working.
* Fall 2007: told her visa was expiring, threatened with deportation or jail unless she went home, paid a $3,000
  renewal fee and $2,000 return fee and came back to the same Florida hotel; she borrowed $3,000 from an aunt against the
  family farm, home and land. She wanted to stay but was told she had no choice. Agency contacted her at least five
  times, warning at least three times she would never work in the US again.
* 2009: same again; threatened with being "blacklisted"; voicemails saying she would be held at the airport if she did
  not pay. Returned October 2009, worked at the Florida hotel until she escaped in April 2010 because she could no
  longer pay placement fees and flights.
* December 2020 psychological evaluation: adjustment disorder with mixed anxiety and depressed mood from the
  trafficking; clinically significant anxiety, nausea, headaches, poor concentration, rumination; ongoing therapy for
  sleep problems and flashbacks; therapist's letter.
* Enrolled in a Trafficking Victims Assistance Program in December 2020 (letter confirming it found her a victim).
* Employment contracts, temporary job offer letters, documents from the recruiters.

Task: assess T-1 eligibility, especially physical presence on account of trafficking given her departures and reentries.
