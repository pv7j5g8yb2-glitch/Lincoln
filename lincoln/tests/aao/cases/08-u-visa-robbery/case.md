# Case: U visa, victim of two armed robberies (Sri Lankan man)

A Sri Lankan man wants to file Form I-918 for U-1 status. An earlier immigrant petition based on his prior marriage
was found to involve a marriage entered into to evade immigration law (INA 204(c) finding).

Facts and evidence:
* Supplement B (law enforcement certification): victim of aggravated robbery on two occasions in 2003, days apart, at
  the gas station where he worked, by the same perpetrator, who pointed a gun at him both times. The injury section
  (part 3.6) is left blank. Police reports record no injuries.
* His statements: the most traumatic experiences of his life; chronic emotional pain, sleeplessness, flashbacks,
  nightmares; became fearful, withdrawn, paranoid; kept working at the station for a time, then quit, took a year off,
  became self employed with no employees because of anxiety. He says the stress aggravated an old spinal injury from a
  fall in Sri Lanka (two year recovery), and he was later diagnosed with a bulging neck disc, herniated back discs and a
  pinched nerve; chronic pain. He was offered counseling but did not go (cost, fear of government programs); a 10 day
  silent meditation retreat brought "a big improvement".
* Physician's letter: treatment recommendations for fibromyalgia, chronic back pain, bulging and degenerative discs; says
  nothing about the robberies.
* Letters from his spouse's parents, sibling and a friend: he became stressed, paranoid, overly cautious of strangers.
* 2015 counselor letter: PTSD from the robberies, sleep disorders, panic attacks; recommends 6 to 9 months of therapy
  twice a week (no sign he went). A second counselor's incomplete email: chronic PTSD.
* 2015 letter from an ayurvedic doctor in Sri Lanka: treating a spinal cord injury since 1998; avoid heavy lifting.

Task: assess eligibility for U nonimmigrant status.
